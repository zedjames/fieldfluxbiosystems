Prospective Health in Organized Dynamical Systems

Lawful stochastic continuation and constitutive maintenance in interacting kinetic populations

Zed James

November 11, 2026DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24143821

Scientific summary

Paper VII carries the prospective Health definition into a specified physical-time interacting system. It constructs a normalized stochastic future law for a finite population of reversible mechanochemical transporters, each with Langevin motion and source-guarded reactions, all exchanging through one shared ionic and chemical inventory.

From a maintained stationary-resource preparation, the paper defines the probability of uninterrupted charge-band survival as a prospective capacity. A second demand requires completion of an accepted transport cycle together with ATP regeneration while the band is maintained. The two requirements distinguish continued operating conditions from accomplished function.

The productive event has strictly positive probability for each finite positive horizon, a fifth-order short-time law, and an attained positive maximum at finite time. The model also proves eventual charge-band exit at every fixed finite population size, with finite expected operating-band lifetime. Under one prepared single-transporter law, strictly positive ATP reserve has zero whole-window capacity while regenerative transport has positive capacity.

An instantaneous report retaining position, phase, chemical inventory, kinetic energy and reaction rates can miss the sign of momentum. That omission preserves the initial band membership and first-order hazard but changes the second-order future likelihood even on the preparation's support. Ideal continuous position observation reconstructs momentum, and permutation of complete transporter labels preserves unlabeled future probabilities.

The mathematics is source-relative. Prospective physiological measurement requires an independently specified observation mechanism, calibration and empirical validation.
