An interacting measurement science

From mathematical definition to observable evidence—and back into inquiry.

Formal structure, physical interpretation, instrument design, and experimental observation are continuously compared. Each has its own standards of evidence; each informs the questions taken up by the others.

  1. 01 / MATHEMATICS

    Define the object

    Specify present realization, viable continuation, requirements, scenarios, and horizons.

  2. 02 / FORMAL METHODS

    Establish the consequences

    Use machine-checkable structures to clarify which distinctions the health question requires.

  3. 03 / PHYSICS

    Identify observables

    Translate requirements into testable hypotheses about boundaries, response, timing, and exchange.

  4. 04 / ENGINEERING

    Build the instrument

    Develop source witness, sensors, calibration, signal acquisition, and traceable computation.

  5. 05 / OBSERVATION

    Acquire the response

    Preserve measured signals, uncertainty, coverage, and provenance across the system.

  6. 06 / EVALUATION

    Test the interpretation

    Compare physical evidence with declared claims; refine models and measurement design where needed.

Two distinct tests: formal verification establishes consequences of the declared mathematics; physical and clinical claims require calibration and empirical validation. Their interaction is the substance of the measurement program.
