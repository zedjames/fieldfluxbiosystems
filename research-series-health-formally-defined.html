Health, Formally Defined

This series develops a mathematical and measurement foundation for health as a prospective property of organized systems: what is presently realized, what can continue under specified conditions, which future capacities are adequate for a declared requirement, and what an observation must preserve to determine that judgment.

Program

Health is treated as a prospective, indexed proposition before it is treated as a measurement.

Paper I begins from a present state and a declared health specification. It separates present constitution from future adequacy and defines prospective health through the normal form present realization + requirement-adequate continuation capacity. Scenario, horizon, and formal requirement remain explicit indices rather than being absorbed into one undifferentiated label.

The response semantics are built upstream of capacity. Lawful histories are restricted by the constitution to the complete viable-continuation family, and capacity is then formed as a downstream observation and collection of that family. This distinction permits the framework to state exactly where information can be compressed and where stronger response semantics are required.

Paper I also constructs the requirement-visible capacity quotient: two capacity values are equivalent exactly when every declared health requirement gives the same adequacy answer on both. The quotient therefore retains precisely the information visible to the declared health language. A universal property shows that every requirement-sufficient representation determines this quotient on its attained range.

The foundational paper ends with explicit boundaries rather than extending its authority beyond the proved layer. Positive prospective capacity does not in general determine failure-sensitive robustness, and observation of present realization alone need not determine prospective health. These stopping points define the work handed forward to later papers.

Paper II takes that fixed-query architecture into changing contexts. It introduces context-specific licensing, health-visible quotients, relation-attained capacity transport, and relation-valued health-class transport; then separates raw-capacity determinacy, health-visible determinacy, and full health truth. Developmental and open-system support fixtures expose where transport is canonical, where it branches or obstructs, and which additional compatibility conditions are required before a health proposition itself can be preserved across context.

What information must be preserved for health to be a well-defined prospective property, and what additional structure is required before that property can be measured, licensed, or used empirically?

Paper III characterizes the representations sufficient to answer a declared health-query language. The canonical query-visible quotient sets the information threshold; a principal refinement theorem describes all sufficient representations. The same order identifies the least sufficient target for exact deterministic transport and the information needed for full state-level health observation. Examples involving control, cost, probability, route structure, and failure-aware robustness show how response semantics can carry these distinctions. Paper IV provides an exact, machine-checked finite forest benchmark for tracing semantic information loss and preserving prior Health judgments under conservative enrichment. Paper V then tests a declared stochastic measurement specification against incomplete Harvard Forest observations, controlled synthetic worlds, and independent mortality outcomes, exposing calibration and decision-reliability limits.

Paper sequence

Published stages of an ongoing health program.

Paper I establishes prospective health through present realization and viable continuation capacity. Paper II develops contextual licensing and exact transport. Paper III characterizes which representations retain enough information for declared health queries, deterministic transport, and state-level observation. Paper IV completes an exact forest provenance and enrichment audit. Paper V develops an auditable forest measurement implementation with independent component calibration and qualified, model-conditional results.

Current horizon

From definition to scientifically licensed measurement.

Papers I–V now connect the prospective-health definition, contextual transport, representation sufficiency, exact semantic provenance, and a real-data-anchored stochastic measurement study. Further work must identify biological entry, calibrate ecological and physiological response laws, resolve near-boundary decision uncertainty, and validate future predictions on independent supports.

Empirical identification remains a separate layer. Biological correctness, physiological sufficiency, diagnostic authority, treatment authority, uncertainty, calibration, and comparison with competing health assessments require evidence beyond theorem proving. The series is designed to make those obligations explicit rather than silently importing them into the foundational definition.
