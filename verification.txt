Verification reality
--------------------
Several statuses that can look similar from outside the repository are kept
distinct in the formal system:

  * source present in the Lean corpus;
  * reachable from a particular import or library root;
  * part of a canonical theorem surface;
  * successfully compiled or checked by a specified verification target;
  * established as a formal mathematical proposition under its stated
    hypotheses and axioms;
  * supported as a scientific interpretation; and
  * supported as an empirical realization.

These are not interchangeable.

A Lean build establishes that the addressed formal objects type-check in the
specified environment. A source inventory establishes what source exists at
the measured snapshot. Neither fact, by itself, establishes an empirical
claim about biology or physics.

Publication-grade formal claims are therefore tied to their actual theorem
statements, dependency surfaces, build/audit targets, and recorded axiom or
hypothesis boundaries rather than inferred from corpus size alone.

What this attests — and what it does not
----------------------------------------
This record attests that, at the measured snapshot, the private Lean source
tree contained the inventory stated above and that the measurement was taken
from a clean main-branch worktree at the recorded commit.

The theorem and lemma counts are syntactic source counts of declarations
beginning with the Lean `theorem` or `lemma` forms under the inventory's
counting rules. They are not a count of independent scientific discoveries,
nor a statement that every declaration belongs to the same verification or
publication class.

Formal verification establishes the exact mathematical propositions proved
by the checked Lean artifacts relative to their stated definitions,
hypotheses, imported foundations, and axioms. Physiological, biological, and
physical interpretations require their own evidentiary and empirical support.
