Constitutive Renewal and Physical Corruption

Transformation-generated residue, renewal resistance, and prospective Health

Zed James

November 27, 2026DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24610039

Scientific summary

Present functional activity can be restored while the conditions of future renewal change. A regenerative mechanochemical transporter can restore present activity while retaining transformation-generated material that restricts subsequent renewal. Matched intact preparations have equal loaded transport rates but strictly graded short-horizon recovery probabilities after a common damage challenge. Exact material accounting, quadratic renewal-response coefficients, an access-disabled control and a requirement-relative Health classification distinguish residual material, renewal resistance and adequacy. The model supplies a prospective measurement specification with controlled probability evaluation and sampling guarantees.

In the specified two-slot model, the short-time recovery coefficient is proportional to (25 − 12b)/32, where b is retained occupancy. Removing the dependence of assembly on vacant slots preserves the projected carrier history despite continuing material retention and clearance.

The result gives a mathematical basis for distinguishing present performance from prospective recovery capacity. Physiological measurement and clinical application require independent physical calibration and validation.
